# Color Theory

## Models
- **RGB/HEX:** screens (additive).
- **CMYK:** print (subtractive); gamut smaller than RGB, so proof.
- **HSL/HSB/OKLCH:** intuitive adjustments; OKLCH gives perceptually even lightness.
- **Pantone/spot:** exact brand colors, special inks.

## Harmony
Monochromatic, analogous, complementary, split-complementary, triadic, tetradic. Start from one dominant hue and keep saturation consistent across the palette.

## Palette construction
- **60/30/10:** dominant, secondary, accent.
- Build **tints and shades** (e.g. 50-900 steps) for each hue.
- Include neutrals (warm or cool, matched to the brand) and functional colors (success, warning, error, info).
- Define dark-mode counterparts, not simple inversion.

## Contrast (WCAG)
| Use                         | Minimum ratio |
|-----------------------------|---------------|
| Body text                   | 4.5:1         |
| Large text (18pt / 14pt bold)| 3:1          |
| UI components, graphics     | 3:1           |
| Enhanced (AAA) body text    | 7:1           |

Check with a contrast tool; test with color-blindness simulation (deuteranopia, protanopia, tritanopia).

## Meaning and culture
Color associations vary by culture and context; verify for target markets. Red can signal danger, celebration or luck; white can mean purity or mourning.

## Practical tips
Use color for emphasis, not decoration; limit accent usage; saturated colors advance, desaturated recede; adjacent colors shift perception, so test in context; keep the same palette across channels, with CMYK/RGB conversions checked.
